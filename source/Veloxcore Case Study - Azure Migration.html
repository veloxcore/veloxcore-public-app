Context

A 6-region platform on decade-old infrastructure

Our client — a logistics platform coordinating freight operations across the UK, Netherlands, Germany, Poland, Spain, and Portugal — had grown through acquisition. Their Azure estate was a patchwork of six separately-managed subscriptions with no unified networking, inconsistent RBAC, and no centralised cost visibility. Engineering spent 30% of its time responding to incidents caused by inter-subscription connectivity failures.

The mandate from the incoming CTO was clear: consolidate to a single, governed Azure landing zone, cut the cloud bill, and do it without taking the platform offline. The business had a hard requirement — no maintenance windows. Freight doesn't stop for infrastructure migrations.

The challenge

Four things that make logistics migrations hard

  • No downtime tolerance: The platform processed live freight manifests 24/7. Even a 10-minute window would cause missed collections across six countries.
  • Tangled dependencies: Workloads across the six subscriptions called each other over public endpoints. No dependency map existed.
  • Compliance variance: GDPR data residency requirements differed by country. Some workloads had to stay in-region; others could consolidate.
  • Six teams, six ways of doing things: Each acquired company had its own deployment process, secret management approach, and monitoring setup. Consolidation meant standardising all of them simultaneously.
Architecture

Hub-spoke landing zone, traffic-shifted migration

We designed a hub-spoke network topology with a single hub VNet hosting shared services — Azure Firewall, Private DNS, VPN Gateway — and six spoke VNets, one per country, connected via VNet peering. All workloads moved to private endpoints; every public endpoint was retired.

The migration used Azure Front Door weighted routing as the traffic shifting mechanism. For each workload, we deployed to the target environment and ran a two-week parallel-run validation — both environments serving identical traffic, with output comparison running automatically. Once parity was confirmed, we shifted 1% of traffic to the new environment, then 10%, then 50%, then 100%. The old environment stayed live as a rollback target until we were confident.

"We went live across six countries with zero downtime. I've never seen a migration that clean — and we've done four before with other vendors."
Sanjay Mehta, VP Engineering · Pan-European Logistics Ltd.

The 40% cost reduction came from three sources: elimination of cross-subscription egress charges (which had been invisible), right-sizing of VMs that were over-provisioned in the acquired companies' estates, and reserved instance commitments on stable workloads. The FinOps dashboard we delivered at the end of the engagement showed cost allocation per workload, per country, per team — for the first time in the company's history.
